# Interpretation of the completed finite plan

Both modules are INCONCLUSIVE. This does not establish that no exit policy can
work; the predefined evidence does not coherently support a provisional choice.
No additional parameter search was run.

## Pure Breakout

At Dynamic 2.5, disabling Trend raises paired mean return by 0.0314 percentage
points, but 23 changed pairs favor ON versus 9 favoring OFF (513 ties).
Removing the largest positive delta reverses the mean; the 10% trim is zero.

Locked versus Dynamic at Trend ON / 2.5 adds 0.5030 percentage points to the
paired mean, with 216 Locked wins / 210 Dynamic wins / 92 ties. It fails the
preregistered symbol-stability gate: only 28/50 symbols favor Locked (56%, below
60%). There are 42 resolution changes, including 41 one-sided fills.
This is MIXED evidence, not a finding that Locked is inferior.

Every larger-multiplier grid comparison has a positive mean B−A but a negative
10% trimmed mean and a paired majority favoring the smaller multiplier. For
2.5 versus 3.0: 0.3419 percentage points mean versus −0.9148 trimmed; 361 pairs
favor 2.5, 64 favor 3.0, 60 tie. Deleting the top three deltas reverses the mean.
2023 supplies 92.7% of positive yearly advantage. There are 62 resolution
changes (61 one-sided fills); at 1.5 versus 2.5 this rises to 126 (124 one-sided).
Aggregate return changes therefore include altered trade sequences.

## Pure Squeeze

Only five of 194 Trend pairs differ, all favoring ON; this is insufficient
informative evidence for the 20-non-tie screen, even though the total pair count
exceeds 100. Trend ON remains a convention for the grid, not a validated choice.

Locked versus Dynamic adds 0.1248 percentage points to paired mean return, but
removing its top three positive deltas reverses the advantage. Four resolution
changes include three one-sided fills.

1.75 beats 2.0 under the descriptive support screen (158/17/26 wins/losses/ties
for 1.75), but it does not also pass its neighboring 1.5 and the 2.5 reference.
That local comparison cannot establish a grid-wide candidate.

3.0 has the highest grid arm mean (1.0832%) and PF (1.3778), but versus 2.5 its
paired mean advantage of 0.6249 percentage points conflicts with a −0.2765
trimmed mean and 125 pairs favoring 2.5 versus 43 favoring 3.0 (18 ties).
2023 supplies 86.2% of positive yearly advantage. This is tail/year-sensitive
and is not promoted solely for its mean, PF or R. Resolution changes range
from zero to eleven across Squeeze comparisons.

## Next evidence and hypotheses

Preserve the two INCONCLUSIVE classifications. Entry research is the next
requested phase, with a finite preregistered plan and explicit exit conventions.
Do not reinterpret the present in-sample results as OOS evidence. Any later
adaptive-exit hypothesis should use signal-time volatility/trend context and
untouched validation data; no such policy was implemented here.
